Reference Snapshots — How platforms show Risk + Performance together

Four product-inspired patterns for surfacing defaults alongside KPIs in a table.

Conceptual recreations based on known design patterns — not pixel-perfect replicas
Reference 01
Salesforce Financial Services Cloud
Account Health Score (circular ring badge) summarises risk into a single number 0–100. Risk flags (red/amber chips) appear inline below the account name. Performance KPIs sit in their own columns but the health score provides the interpretive frame first.

What to take from Salesforce: The Health Score ring is the single number that contextualises all other data on the row. A financier sees 22/100 before reading any MTD/YTD figure — so the defaults don't have to compete with positive KPIs, they've already been factored into a score. Risk flags below the name reinforce the "why" without cluttering the metric columns.
Reference 02
Bloomberg Terminal — Credit Risk Screen
Dark interface with strict colour coding: green = positive, red = negative, amber = warning. Risk metrics get their own column group separated by a visual divider. No chips or badges — the numbers and their colours are the entire language. Default probability shown as a percentage, not a count.

What to take from Bloomberg: Risk gets its own column group, separated by a visual divider line. The eye naturally groups "performance" vs "risk" as two distinct zones on the same row. Default history shown as 4× — compact, factual, no labels needed. The DRSK score (0–100, high = risky) converts complexity into one scannable number per row.
Reference 03
Experian / CIBIL Business Credit Report
Credit score displayed as a circular badge — the leftmost element after the name. DPD (Days Past Due) shows the default timeline as coloured dots (red = default month, green = clean month). Score bands: 700+ Excellent · 600–699 Good · 500–599 Fair · Below 500 Poor.

What to take from CIBIL: The DPD dot timeline is powerful — 12 coloured dots (one per month) show the entire default history at a glance without any numbers. A row of red dots tells a story no count can. "4 times" as a number is abstract; 4 red dots scattered across 12 months is visceral. Consider this for your drawer — not the table, but the widget.
Reference 04
SAP Credit Management
Risk Class is a categorical field (A = lowest risk → D = highest risk), not a score. Days in Arrears shown as a number with colour. Credit exposure visualised as a bar. Clean separation: limit management columns on the left, risk class and arrears on the right.

What to take from SAP: Risk Class A/B/C/D is categorical — not a score, not a count. It abstracts all risk signals (defaults, arrears, exposure) into a single letter. A financier learns once what A/B/C/D means and never has to re-interpret. "Days in Arrears + occurrences" as two lines in one cell is the cleanest way to show default severity + frequency together without adding two columns.
